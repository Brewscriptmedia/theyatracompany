02 — THE MODEL

How search and answer engines actually decide

Every fix below traces back to one of these five mechanics. Learn the mechanics and you stop needing checklists.

1. Crawl → Render → Index → Rank → Present

Five separate gates. A page can pass four and fail the fifth. Most "SEO problems" are actually one specific gate failing:

  • Crawl — can a bot reach the URL? (links, sitemap, robots)
  • Render — is the content in the HTML? (yours: yes, RSC-rendered)
  • Index — is it worth storing? (uniqueness, depth, duplication)
  • Rank — is it the best answer? (relevance, links, experience signals)
  • Present — does anyone click? (title, description, rich results, OG)

Your site fails hardest at Crawl (orphaned guides) and Present (no price in titles, broken rich-result eligibility).

2. Answer engines retrieve passages, not pages

ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and AI Overviews chunk your page, embed each chunk, retrieve the best-matching chunks, then synthesise. Consequences:

  • A chunk must be self-contained. "It costs ₹2,499" is useless out of context; "A Lucknow to Ayodhya sedan taxi costs ₹2,499 one-way" is citable.
  • Numbers, named entities and units raise retrieval scores. Vague adjectives lower them.
  • Short, declarative, question-shaped headings create clean chunk boundaries.
  • Tables and lists survive chunking better than long prose paragraphs.

3. Entities, not keywords

Google resolves your site to an entity — a business with a name, address, phone, service area and reputation — and then decides which queries that entity deserves. Every inconsistency (Chinhat vs BBD Green City, "Across India" vs "Lucknow", 4.9★ vs 9 reviews vs "5000+ customers") weakens entity confidence. Consistency is a ranking input, not a tidiness preference.

4. Internal links are how you spend authority

Your homepage accumulates whatever authority the domain has. Links distribute it. A page with no inbound internal links receives roughly nothing and is crawled rarely. Right now every guide is one hop from a single hub page and links to nothing — you have built ten cul-de-sacs. Anchor text is also a direct relevance signal: Read Guide → transfers zero meaning; Lucknow to Ayodhya taxi fares transfers exactly the right meaning.

5. Search Experience Optimisation (SXO)

Google measures whether the click worked. Slow LCP, layout shift, a CTA that opens a blocked popup, a form with no labels — these are ranking-relevant because they produce pogo-sticking. For a phone-first taxi business on mobile 4G, the CTA is the SEO. A page that ranks #1 and converts nothing will not stay at #1.

6. Proof beats assertion

"Trusted", "Reliable", "Professional" are unfalsifiable and carry no weight with users, Google, or an LLM summarising you. Falsifiable claims do: "₹2,499 fixed one-way — toll, driver allowance and GST included; nothing added at drop-off." That sentence can be quoted by an AI, compared by a user, and rewarded by Google. Every adjective in your copy should be replaced by a number, a name, or a policy.

HighTEC-01Google Fonts import is misspelled twice — the font never loads and the request is wasted▶

Evidence

/* src/app/globals.css line 1 */
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Poopins:wght@300;400;500;600;700;800&displey=swap');

body{ font-family: 'Poopins', sans-serif; }

Poopins should be Poppins. displey should be display. The family does not exist, so the request fails and font-family:'Poopins' resolves to nothing.

Why it matters

Three separate costs. (1) A render-blocking stylesheet request to a third-party origin on every page load — CSS @import is serialised after the parent stylesheet downloads, which is the slowest possible way to load a font. (2) It fails, so you pay the latency and get nothing. (3) You already load Geist via next/font/google in layout.js, which self-hosts the font, preloads it, and eliminates layout shift. You are running two font strategies and the broken one is the blocking one.

Principle — Never load fonts with CSS @import. In Next.js, next/font self-hosts, sets font-display:swap, emits a preload, and computes a size-adjusted fallback so text does not reflow. Anything else is a regression.

Fix

  • Delete the @import line entirely.
  • Pick one typeface. If you want Poppins, swap the next/font/google import in layout.js from Geist to Poppins with explicit weights.
  • Replace body{font-family:'Poopins',sans-serif} with the CSS variable next/font exposes.

12 — PLAYBOOKS

Page playbooks to bake into your workflow

Three templates: route/guide pages, blog posts, and landing pages. Each is a rule set, not a checklist — the reasoning is included so you can adapt it.

Rules that apply to every page you will ever publish

  • One intent per URL. If a page targets both "Lucknow to Ayodhya taxi" and "Ayodhya to Lucknow taxi", it will underperform on both. Split it.
  • Answer-first. The first 60 words state the answer with numbers and entity names, quotable with no surrounding context.
  • Title budget. ≤40 characters before the brand template. Primary keyword in the first 30. One pipe maximum. Include a number where the query is commercial.
  • Description. 150–160 characters, one number, one call to action, primary keyword present.
  • One h1, strict heading order. Headings phrased as the questions a reader would actually ask, because those become the chunk boundaries.
  • Three internal links minimum in the body, with descriptive anchor text. Up to the hub, sideways to siblings, down to conversion.
  • Canonical set explicitly via alternates.canonical. Never rely on inference.
  • Schema matches what is visible. Every marked-up property has an on-page counterpart.
  • One priority image per page via next/image with explicit dimensions, under 200 KB delivered.
  • Generated OG image at 1200×630, under 300 KB, with the page's key number rendered into it.
  • Added to the sitemap with a real lastmod, and linked from at least one existing page before publishing.
  • A conversion event on every CTA, with a unique position identifier.

A. Route / destination guide

Intent: transactional with heavy research. The reader wants a fare and wants to believe it.

Section order — this order is the point:

  1. H1 with route + vehicle-agnostic fare hook
  2. Byline + "fares verified {date}"
  3. Answer block (45–70 words, all numbers)
  4. Key facts list (4 bullets)
  5. Quick info grid — distance, time, route, fare range, best time
  6. Fare table with per-vehicle pricing + breakdown + Offer schema
  7. CTA #1 — "Lock this fare on WhatsApp"
  8. What's included / not included
  9. Route detail — highway, tolls, stops, road condition, tips
  10. Destination detail — about, why visit, attractions, food, climate
  11. Nearest airport / railway station (entity anchors)
  12. Why book direct (positioning block)
  13. Attributed reviews for this route
  14. FAQs — 6–8, each 40–80 words
  15. CTA #2 + related routes + nearby destinations

Length: 1,200–1,800 words. Non-negotiable: every number must exist in the data file, never in prose only.

B. Blog post

Intent: informational, top of funnel. Its job is to earn topical authority and to feed the route pages — not to convert directly.

  1. H1 phrased as the reader's question
  2. Byline + published + updated dates
  3. Answer block — the full answer in 60 words. Do not withhold it; withholding costs the featured snippet and gains nothing.
  4. Table of contents for posts over 1,000 words
  5. Body in h2 sections, each a distinct sub-question, 150–300 words each
  6. At least one table or comparison — the most extractable format there is
  7. One relevant, non-forced link to a route guide per major section
  8. FAQ block of 4–6 questions not already answered in the body
  9. Soft CTA at the end, tied to the topic

Topic test: if the post cannot naturally link to at least two route or service pages, it is off-strategy — write a different post.

Schema: BlogPosting with author, datePublished, dateModified, image, mentions; plus FAQPage for the FAQ block; plus BreadcrumbList.

C. Service / landing page

Intent: commercial. One service, one audience, one action.

  1. H1 = service + city. No cleverness.
  2. Sub-headline with the starting price and what it includes
  3. Proof row — rating with count, years, routes, reply time
  4. Primary CTA above the fold, repeated as a sticky bar on mobile
  5. Answer block defining the service in 50 words
  6. What's included / what's not — the objection-handling block
  7. Pricing table with Offer schema
  8. How it works — 4 steps, one sentence each
  9. Vehicles available with capacity
  10. Attributed reviews specific to this service
  11. FAQs — 6+, objection-shaped: cancellation, payment, waiting, luggage, delays
  12. Final CTA + links to related routes

Landing-page discipline: exactly one conversion action. No social icons, no newsletter, no outbound links above the fold. Every element either supports the booking decision or is deleted.

The AEO layer — apply to all three

  • Chunk hygiene. Every h2 section should stand alone if quoted. No "as mentioned above", no pronouns referring to earlier sections.
  • Numbers over adjectives. Ban "comfortable", "reliable", "best", "trusted", "affordable" from answer blocks and FAQ answers.
  • Restate the subject. First sentence of every section repeats the entity: "The Lucknow to Ayodhya route…" not "This route…".
  • Tables for comparisons. They survive chunking and are disproportionately quoted.
  • Entity anchors. Name the airport, the highway, the temple, the station in full, at least once each.
  • Dates on anything with a price. Undated prices get hedged or skipped by AI answers.
  • Consistency across surfaces. Page copy, JSON-LD, llms.txt and GBP must not disagree on any fact.
